Artifact-to-execution assurance for canonical integer GBDTs

GL1F serializes integer-quantized gradient-boosted trees into a canonical binary form that can be reconstructed from chain state and evaluated through Solidity, JavaScript, Python, and C++. The protocol intentionally publishes its model bytes, and its claims are classified by proof, executable evidence, benchmark, or explicit scope statement.

Claim discipline

What the evidence establishes

Formal supplement →
✓

Established within stated conditions

  • Canonical v1 and v2 byte layouts have exact finite sizes.
  • Valid fixed-depth traversal terminates and selects one leaf per tree.
  • Specification-level integer inference is equivalent under the stated reconstruction and execution premises.
  • Twenty-five distinct recorded training profiles produced identical JavaScript, Python, and C++ cores; a separate arithmetic witness exercises the operation-order boundary.
  • The six-shape scalar EVM sweep produced 72/72 exact reference/runtime outputs.
  • All 12 active records at the pinned block passed the documented reconstruction checks.
  • For the pinned provider-attested record, nine deterministic vectors per active model yielded 108/108 matching local and returned call results.
≠

Not established by the protocol

  • Predictive accuracy or generalization on an unspecified dataset.
  • Truth, provenance, causality, authorship, fairness, or safety of a model.
  • Confidentiality of published weights or exclusive local inference.
  • Consensus finality for an unpinned eth_call response.
  • Integrity of every future registry entry without independent validation.

Protocol boundary

Open by design. Reconstructible by specification.

Published model bytes are intentionally readable so anyone can reconstruct and reevaluate them. Optional service paths do not create weight confidentiality. Exact execution identity is distinct from model accuracy, dataset provenance, and application fitness.
